### Truth & Reconciliation

1. Truth comes before reconciliation. The Truth and Reconciliation Commission collected 7,000 statements and more than 5 million records to understand the truth first before making any recommendations.

2. What residential schools were. Approximately 150,000 children attended residential schools, with the last one closing only 30 years ago in 1996. Residential schools were later called “cultural genocide” by the TRC.

3. Reconciliation is a relationship. The TRC considers reconciliation as building a relationship between Indigenous and non-Indigenous peoples that is founded on mutual respect.

### Call to Action 92

1. We assumed the 94 Calls to Action were actively being addressed. However, we learned that 41% of the calls have not started or have stalled in progress. Call to Action 92 is among those not yet addressed. 

2. Diversity language is not enough. “We value diversity” or “we welcome everyone” doesn’t prohibit anything. Even “equal opportunity employer” is only a general commitment. 

3. Indigenous identity isn’t its own ground in the Ontario Human Rights Code. It’s protected through ancestry, place of origin, ethnic origin, race, colour and creed. Naming First Nations, Inuit and Métis explicitly is a standard a company can choose to adopt.

4. Good intentions can break the law. “Indigenous candidates preferred” with no legal basis can itself be discrimination. Indigenous-specific hiring needs a section 14 special program, or section 24, which is narrow and only for organizations primarily serving Indigenous interests.

5. Hiring policies aren't enough on their own. Retention and advancement policies and actions are also required.
Formally published documentation, sponsored by leadership shows your commitment. leadership-sponsored mentorship beats informal arrangements. Objective, skills-based and transparent promotion criteria remove gatekeeping. 

6. Not all criteria weigh the same. Some are law, some only apply in certain cases, and some are good practice. This became the ×3 / ×2 / ×1 tiers.
